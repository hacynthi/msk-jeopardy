options:
  A: Extension teardrop fracture at C2; typically stable, seen in elderly
  B: Clay-shoveler fracture (spinous process avulsion); no cord involvement
  C: Flexion teardrop fracture at C5; associated with anterior cord syndrome
  D: Burst fracture of C5; may spare the posterior column
explanation: >
  The **flexion teardrop fracture** is one of the **most unstable and devastating**
  cervical injuries. A triangular anteroinferior vertebral body fragment is sheared
  off by hyperflexion while the posterior body is **retropulsed into the spinal
  canal**. The result is near-universal **anterior cord syndrome**: bilateral
  paralysis and loss of pain/temperature sensation with **preserved proprioception
  and vibration** (posterior columns intact). It must be distinguished from the
  **extension teardrop** (C2, elderly, avulsion by anterior longitudinal ligament,
  generally stable with normal neurology). Any cervical diving injury with
  quadriplegia should raise this diagnosis immediately.
