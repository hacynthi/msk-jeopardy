options:
  A: Greenstick fracture (one cortex breaks, opposite cortex bows)
  B: Buckle (torus) fracture (cortical buckling without a complete fracture line)
  C: Salter-Harris II fracture (fracture through physis and metaphysis)
  D: Plastic bowing deformity (bowing without any cortical disruption)
explanation: >
  A **buckle (torus) fracture** is the **most common pediatric fracture**. The
  immature cortical bone compresses and **buckles outward** at the metaphysis
  under axial load without a complete fracture line. It is a **stable** injury
  requiring only splinting. On radiograph, look for a subtle cortical irregularity
  or bump at the distal radial metaphysis. Do not confuse with a greenstick (one
  cortex disrupted) or a Salter-Harris fracture (involves the growth plate).
